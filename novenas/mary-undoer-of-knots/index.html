Novena to Our Lady, Undoer of Knots

Prayed to
The Blessed Virgin Mary, Undoer of Knots
Intentions
Difficult relationships and family conflict, Trust in God, Impossible causes, Desperate situations

Pray once a day for nine days: the opening prayer, then the day's reflection, then the closing prayer. Hold one intention through all nine days.

Source

Built from two prayers of verified ancient, public-domain standing — the Sub Tuum Praesidium (c. 250 AD, the oldest known prayer addressed to Mary, attested on Rylands Papyrus 470) and the Memorare (traditional, medieval) — combined with original meditation text on the documented history of the Augsburg devotion. The popular modern 'Undoer of Knots novena' prayer circulating online is deliberately not reproduced here, as its authorship and date could not be verified as pre-1923. The opening prayer is the Sub Tuum Praesidium; the days close by alternating it with the Memorare. The devotion's own history is recorded here as background rather than repeated daily as the prayer text: around 1700, Fr. Hieronymus Ambrosius Langenmantel of Augsburg commissioned a painting of Mary untying knots in a ribbon, after a Jesuit priest's prayer over a troubled marriage's wedding ribbon was credited with healing it; the devotion reached a wider audience after Jorge Mario Bergoglio — later Pope Francis — encountered the image while studying in Germany in 1986. As early as the second century, St. Irenaeus of Lyons wrote that 'the knot of Eve's disobedience was loosed by the obedience of Mary.'

Day 1 of 9

The Knot of Fear

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Every account of this devotion begins with a marriage that seemed to be coming apart — not through one dramatic rupture, but through the slow accumulation of small tangles: unspoken resentments, misread intentions, the ordinary wear that turns two people who chose each other into two people who avoid each other. Fear is usually the thread underneath: fear of being unloved, of having failed, of a conversation that might make things worse instead of better. Fear tightens a knot rather than loosening it, because a frightened hand pulls instead of working the strand free. Bring to Mary today whatever relationship — a marriage, a friendship, a bond with a parent or a child — has a fear like that running under it. Ask not for the other person to change on your schedule, but for your own hands to stop pulling, so that whatever is tangled has room to be worked loose rather than yanked tighter.

Day 2 of 9

The Knot of Pride

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Pride rarely announces itself as pride. It usually shows up disguised as being right, as keeping score, as waiting for the other person to apologize first. It is one of the oldest knots there is — Irenaeus traced the whole tangle back to a first act of self-will in the garden, a reaching for something not meant to be reached for alone. Mary's answer to the angel, 'be it done to me according to thy word' (Luke 1:38), is the opposite motion: not self-assertion but surrender, and the tradition holds that this surrender is exactly what begins undoing the earlier knot. If there is a relationship where you are waiting to be right before you'll be reconciled, sit with that today. Ask Mary's help not to win the disagreement but to want the relationship more than you want to be vindicated in it — which is very often the actual knot standing in the way.

Closing prayer

Day 3 of 9

The Knot of Unforgiveness

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

An unforgiven wound is a knot pulled so tight it starts to feel like part of the rope itself — you stop imagining the ribbon could ever lie flat again. Forgiveness does not require pretending the injury didn't happen, any more than untying a knot means pretending the ribbon was never tangled. It means being willing to work at it anyway, strand by strand, without demanding the other person go first. Christ's own words from the cross — 'Father, forgive them, for they know not what they do' (Luke 23:34) — were spoken before any apology was offered, while the wrong was still actively being done. That is the model this devotion asks us to imitate: forgiveness offered while the knot is still visibly there, not after it has conveniently vanished. Name, if only to yourself, the specific unforgiveness sitting in your chest today, and ask for the grace to begin, even slightly, loosening your grip on it.

Day 4 of 9

The Knot of Silence

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Some of the worst knots in a family are made of the things nobody said. A conversation avoided for years, a subject that gets steered around at every gathering, a hurt that was never named out loud so it was never actually addressed — silence like this doesn't dissolve a tangle, it just hides it under more thread. Mary, at the wedding at Cana, did the opposite: she noticed a quiet problem — the wine had run out, an embarrassment nobody wanted to say aloud — and she simply named it plainly to her Son (John 2:3). Small as it sounds, that is the pattern: not confrontation, but honest, unafraid naming of what is actually wrong. If there is a silence in one of your relationships that has calcified into distance, ask today for the courage to say the true thing gently, rather than let the knot keep growing unseen under a fabric of politeness.

Day 5 of 9

The Knot of Old Wounds

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Some knots are old enough that you've half-forgotten they're there, and yet they still shape the shape of the rope — a childhood disappointment, a betrayal years back, a grief that was never properly mourned. These are often the hardest knots to work loose precisely because they are so familiar; a person can build an entire identity around an old wound without noticing. The devotion's founding story is itself about an old, entrenched trouble, not a fresh one — a marriage already worn down by years of accumulated friction before anyone thought to bring it to Mary. That is worth remembering when a knot feels too old, too set, too much a part of you to imagine differently. Bring to prayer today whatever old wound still quietly governs how you love or trust, and ask that it be looked at directly, gently, and finally begun to be undone rather than simply carried.

Day 6 of 9

The Knot of Comparison

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Jealousy and comparison knot relationships from the outside in: the sibling measured against another, the spouse compared to someone else's marriage, the quiet resentment of a friend's good fortune. It is a knot made of looking sideways instead of at the person actually in front of you. Mary's own song, the Magnificat, is strikingly free of this — she rejoices in what God has done for her without needing it measured against anyone else's portion, and she blesses a God who 'hath regarded the humility of his handmaid' rather than her rank among others (Luke 1:48). If comparison has tangled a relationship in your life — a rivalry, a resentment, a habit of measuring your worth or someone else's against a third party — bring that specifically today. Ask for the freedom to see the person in front of you clearly, on their own terms, rather than through the knot of who else they are being weighed against.

Day 7 of 9

The Knot of Despair

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

The most dangerous knot of all is the belief that a particular tangle is simply beyond undoing — that this marriage, this estrangement, this situation is now permanent, and hoping otherwise is naive. Despair doesn't fight the knot; it stops trying, which is worse. The whole reason this devotion exists is a direct answer to that belief: a marriage that seemed finished was not finished, once someone was willing to bring it, unresolved and unimproved, to prayer rather than to resignation. Nothing about the ribbon in the painting is described as easy to untangle — Mary is shown working at it patiently, strand by strand, not waving it straight. If despair has crept into how you see a particular relationship or situation — the quiet sense that nothing will change, so why keep trying — name that despair honestly today, and ask, specifically, for hope to be restored before asking for the situation itself to change.

Closing prayer

We fly to thy patronage, O holy Mother of God; despise not our petitions in our necessities, but deliver us always from all dangers, O glorious and blessed Virgin. Amen. (Sub Tuum Praesidium, c. 250 AD)

Day 8 of 9

The Knot of Division

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Some knots are not between two people but woven through a whole family or community — a rift that has split people into sides, a falling-out that pulled others in, a wound that widened until nearly everyone was standing somewhere in relation to it. These are the hardest knots to name because so many hands, over time, added to the tangle without meaning to. Christ prayed, on the night before He died, that His followers 'may be one' (John 17:21) — not identical, not agreed on everything, but bound rather than scattered. If there is a wider division in your family, your parish, your circle of friends that you find yourself caught inside, bring the whole tangled shape of it here today, not just your own thread of it. Ask for the grace to be someone who works toward unity rather than someone who, even with good intentions, keeps pulling the knot tighter.

Day 9 of 9

The Ribbon Made Straight

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

In the Augsburg painting, the ribbon does not end in Mary's hands, still tangled — it passes through them and out the other side, smooth, into the hands of a second angel, ready to be carried on. That is the whole point of the image: her role is not to hold the knot forever but to work it loose so that life can continue past it. The marriage the devotion commemorates did not stay in crisis; it was healed and went on to be lived. Whatever knot you have named across these nine days — fear, pride, unforgiveness, silence, an old wound, comparison, despair, division — bring it to this final day not as something to keep worrying at, but as something you are handing over, trusting it will keep being worked on even after this novena ends. Give thanks today for whatever has already begun to loosen, even slightly, and ask for the patience to trust the rest to her hands.

Closing prayer

We fly to thy patronage, O holy Mother of God; despise not our petitions in our necessities, but deliver us always from all dangers, O glorious and blessed Virgin. Amen. (Sub Tuum Praesidium, c. 250 AD)

About The Blessed Virgin Mary, Undoer of Knots

A devotional title of the Blessed Virgin Mary, not a separate figure or a canonized saint. It names her intercession under the image of untying knots — the tangles of fear, pride, and estrangement that bind a marriage or a family. The title traces to an eighteenth-century painting in Augsburg, Germany, commissioned to ask Mary's help for one troubled marriage, and was carried to the wider Church after a young Argentine priest named Jorge Mario Bergoglio — later Pope Francis — encountered it and brought a copy home. Feast_date is left null: this is a private devotion, not a date on the universal Church calendar.
