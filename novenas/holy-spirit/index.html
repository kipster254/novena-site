Novena to the Holy Spirit

Prayed to
The Holy Spirit
Intentions
The gifts and fruits of the Holy Spirit, Guidance and discernment, Vocations, Trust in God

Pray once a day for nine days: the opening prayer, then the day's reflection, then the closing prayer. Hold one intention through all nine days.

Source

Rooted in Acts 1:12-14 and Acts 2 (Douay-Rheims translation). Closing prayers are Edward Caswall's 1849 English translations (Lyra Catholica) of the Veni Creator Spiritus (9th century, attributed to Rabanus Maurus) and the Veni Sancte Spiritus, the 'Golden Sequence' (13th century, attributed to Stephen Langton) — both hymn and translation long in the public domain. The opening prayer is the traditional 'Come, Holy Spirit' invocation — the versicle 'Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created' with its response 'And Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.' This novena's own history is recorded here as background rather than repeated daily as the prayer text: it reaches back to the nine days between the Ascension and Pentecost recorded in Acts 1-2, which is also why its traditional calendar dates shift with Easter every year rather than sitting on a fixed MM-DD.

Day 1 of 9

The Upper Room

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

Before there was fire, there was waiting. The apostles had just watched Christ ascend, and instead of being told what would happen next, they were told to wait for it — to return to the city and pray until 'the promise of the Father' arrived (Acts 1:4). They did not know the day or the hour. They only knew to gather, and to stay together, and Mary stayed with them: the one member of that room who had already carried the Holy Spirit within her, at the Annunciation, and who now waited alongside the rest for Him to come again in a new way. That waiting is itself worth entering. A novena is not a formula for producing an outcome on schedule; it is nine days of staying in the room, in the prayer, in the company of others who are waiting too — for clarity, for peace, for the courage a task or a relationship or a fear requires. Whatever brought you here, let today simply be the choice to show up and wait with attention, the way that first room did.

Closing prayer

Come, O Creator Spirit blest, And in our souls take up Thy rest; Come, with Thy grace and heavenly aid, To fill the hearts which Thou hast made. (Veni Creator Spiritus, 9th c., trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 2 of 9

Wisdom

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

The first of the seven gifts is wisdom, and it is easy to mistake for cleverness — it is not. Wisdom, in the classical sense the Church means, is a taste for the things of God: not merely knowing that God exists or what He asks, but relishing it, judging everyday matters the way someone who loves God would judge them. It is the gift that lets a person look at a hard situation — a decision, a loss, a temptation — and see it, even briefly, from a vantage point larger than their own anxiety. Solomon's famous prayer was for exactly this: not riches, not long life, but 'an understanding heart' to judge rightly (1 Kings 3:9). Ask today for that same taste, that same larger vantage point, over whatever in your life currently looks too tangled or too small to see clearly. Wisdom does not always arrive as an answer. Sometimes it arrives as the willingness to set the question down and trust the One who already sees the whole of it.

Closing prayer

Great Comforter, to Thee we cry; O highest gift of God most high, O Fount of life, O Fire of love, And sweet anointing from above! (Veni Creator Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 3 of 9

Understanding

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

Understanding is the gift of penetrating beneath the surface of a truth to grasp what it actually means for a life. Two people can hear the same teaching, the same Gospel line, the same piece of counsel from a friend, and only one of them is changed by it — because only one of them was given, in that moment, to understand it, not just to hear it. The disciples on the road to Emmaus had heard the whole story of the Passion and still walked away sorrowful and blind, until 'their eyes were opened' (Luke 24:31) and the same facts became, suddenly, everything. Pray today for that kind of opening — not new information, but a deeper reading of what you already know: about your faith, about the people you love, about the situation weighing on you. Ask the Spirit to move a truth from your head into the part of you that actually lives by it.

Closing prayer

The sacred sevenfold grace is Thine, Dread finger of the hand divine; The promise of the Father Thou, Who dost the tongue with power endow. (Veni Creator Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 4 of 9

Counsel

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

Counsel is the gift for the actual moment of decision — not abstract principle, but this choice, today, in front of you. It is often described as the Spirit acting like a good and trusted advisor seated close enough to speak quietly at the exact moment you need to hear something. Christ promised His followers this directly, when facing trial and interrogation: 'it shall be given you in that hour what to speak... for it is not you that speak, but the Spirit of your Father that speaketh in you' (Matthew 10:19-20). Few of us stand before tribunals, but all of us stand, regularly, at forks: a word said or withheld, a path taken or declined, a vocation weighed. If a decision is pressing on you now — large or small — bring it here specifically. Ask not for the situation to be simplified, but for the counsel to see clearly what is being asked of you inside it, and the readiness to act on what you're shown.

Closing prayer

Kindle our senses from above, And make our hearts o'erflow with love; With patience firm, and virtue high, The weakness of our flesh supply. (Veni Creator Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 5 of 9

Fortitude

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

Look at the apostles twice in the same book of Scripture. Before Pentecost, on the night of the arrest, they scatter, and Peter — the one who would become the rock — denies even knowing Christ, three times, to a servant girl. After Pentecost, that same Peter stands in front of the very council that condemned Jesus and says plainly, 'we ought to obey God, rather than men' (Acts 5:29), and is beaten for it, and leaves 'rejoicing that they were accounted worthy to suffer' (5:41). Nothing about Peter's temperament changed between those two scenes. What changed was the gift of fortitude: strength that does not depend on personal courage, because it isn't personal. Fortitude is not the absence of fear; it is the capacity to act rightly in spite of it, sustained by something larger than your own nerve. If there is something you know you should do or say and haven't, because it costs something, ask for this gift specifically today.

Closing prayer

Far from us drive the foe we dread, And grant us Thy true peace instead; So shall we not, with Thee for guide, Turn from the path of life aside. (Veni Creator Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 6 of 9

Knowledge

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

The gift of knowledge, in this classical list, is not information — it is the ability to see created things, events, and other people rightly in relation to God: to recognize what draws you toward Him and what quietly draws you away, even when both look harmless on the surface. It is the gift that lets someone notice, honestly, that a habit, a friendship, an ambition, or a grief has become something it was never meant to be. The author of Ecclesiastes had this gift in view when he wrote of a right season for everything under heaven (Ecclesiastes 3:1) — the discernment to read one's own life truthfully rather than through the story we'd prefer to tell about it. Ask today for that plain, unflinching kind of sight regarding your own circumstances: not judgment of others, but honest clarity about where you actually stand, so that whatever comes next is built on what's real.

Closing prayer

O may Thy grace on us bestow The Father and the Son to know, And evermore to hold confessed Thyself of each the Spirit blest. Amen. (Veni Creator Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 7 of 9

Piety

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

Piety, in the seven gifts, means something warmer than the word now suggests — not stiff observance, but the affection of a child for a father. It is the gift that makes prayer feel like coming home rather than reporting for duty, and it is why the room in Acts 1 held not only the eleven apostles but 'the women, and Mary the mother of Jesus' — people whose presence in prayer was not obligation but belonging. Piety is what lets someone return, day after day in a novena like this one, without needing to force it: a settled tenderness toward God that survives dry spells and distraction. If prayer currently feels effortful or distant, that is not a failure; ask instead, honestly, for this specific gift — the restoration of the sense that you are welcome in this conversation, not performing it. Piety toward God tends, too, to soften how we treat the people around us, since both flow from the same recognition of belonging to someone.

Closing prayer

Light immortal! Light divine! Visit Thou these hearts of Thine, And our inmost being fill; Where Thou art not, man hath naught, Nothing good in deed or thought, Nothing free from taint of ill. (Veni Sancte Spiritus, 'the Golden Sequence,' 13th c., trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 8 of 9

Fear of the Lord

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

This is the gift most often misunderstood, because 'fear' in the modern sense means dread, and that is not what is meant here. Fear of the Lord is reverence — the awe of standing before something immeasurably greater than yourself, and the corresponding horror of wounding, carelessly, someone who loves you completely. Proverbs calls it 'the beginning of wisdom' (9:10), not because terror teaches anything, but because reverence is the posture that finally lets a person be taught at all. It is the opposite of the presumption that treats holiness casually or grace as something owed. Ask today for that grounded awe — not anxiety, but the sober recognition of who God is and who you are before Him — trusting that this reverence, rightly held, does not shrink a person but steadies them, the way respect for the sea steadies a sailor rather than keeping him off the water.

Closing prayer

Heal our wounds, our strength renew; On our dryness pour Thy dew; Wash the stains of guilt away; Bend the stubborn heart and will; Melt the frozen, warm the chill; Guide the steps that go astray. (Veni Sancte Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

Day 9 of 9

Fire and Wind

Opening prayer

Come, Holy Spirit, fill the hearts of Thy faithful, and kindle in them the fire of Thy love. Send forth Thy Spirit, and they shall be created, and Thou shalt renew the face of the earth.

On the ninth day, the waiting ended. 'There came a sound from heaven, as of a mighty wind coming... and there appeared to them parted tongues, as it were of fire' (Acts 2:2-3), and the room that had held frightened, uncertain people for nine days sent out, within the hour, men who preached boldly in languages they had never learned, and three thousand were baptized before the sun set. That is the pattern this novena has followed: not nine days of striving to earn something, but nine days of staying present so that something could be given. Whatever gift or grace has been the quiet thread of your prayer this week, ask today that it bear the ordinary fruit Scripture names as the sign the Spirit has truly taken root: 'charity, joy, peace, patience, benignity, goodness, longanimity, mildness, faith, modesty, continency, chastity' (Galatians 5:22-23, Douay-Rheims). Let the fire settle into daily life, not just today's feeling.

Closing prayer

Thou, on those who evermore Thee confess and Thee adore, In Thy sevenfold gifts, descend: Give them comfort when they die, Give them life with Thee on high, Give them joys that never end. (Veni Sancte Spiritus, trans. Edward Caswall, 1849)

About The Holy Spirit

The Third Person of the Blessed Trinity, true God equal to the Father and the Son, sent upon the apostles at Pentecost. This entry is not a canonized saint but the object of the Church's oldest novena: the nine days of prayer kept by the apostles and Our Lady in the upper room between the Ascension and Pentecost (Acts 1:12-14), the pattern every later novena imitates. Feast_date is left null because Pentecost is a movable feast tied to Easter and cannot be expressed as a fixed calendar date.

Acts 1:12-14 and Acts 2 (Douay-Rheims); Veni Creator Spiritus, 9th century, attributed to Rabanus Maurus; Veni Sancte Spiritus ('the Golden Sequence'), 13th century, attributed to Stephen Langton, Archbishop of Canterbury; both hymns in the English translation of Edward Caswall, Lyra Catholica (1849) — all public domain.
