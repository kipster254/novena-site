Opening prayer

Pray this at the start of each day.

O Holy St. Jude, Apostle and Martyr, great in virtue and rich in miracles, near kinsman of Jesus Christ, faithful intercessor of all who invoke your special patronage in time of need: to you I have recourse from the depth of my heart, and humbly beg you, to whom God has given such great power, to come to my assistance. Help me in my present and urgent petition. In return, I promise to make your name known and cause you to be invoked. St. Jude, pray for us and for all who honor thee and invoke thy aid. Amen.

Day 1 of 9

A Kinsman of the Lord

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Tradition holds that Jude was a relative of Jesus — a kinsman by blood, son of Cleophas and Mary, cousin to the Lord he would follow. But blood alone made him no more a disciple than anyone else; even sharing a family with the Savior, he still had to choose, each day, to believe and to follow. That is worth sitting with as this novena opens: nearness to Christ is never inherited, only chosen, and chosen again. Whatever has brought you to ask Jude's help today, begin by asking simply for the grace to draw nearer to Christ yourself, however hopeless the rest looks from here.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, kinsman of the Lord, pray for us.

Day 2 of 9

Chosen From the Ordinary

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Scripture says almost nothing about Jude next to Peter, John, or Paul. He is named once in the list of the Twelve and then largely vanishes from the record — an ordinary Galilean, called by Christ for no distinction the Gospels bother to mention. God has always worked this way: through the overlooked, the unremarkable, the person nobody would have picked. You do not need a great reputation, a strong case, or an impressive history for your petition to matter to God. Bring today whatever feels too small, too ordinary, or too unlikely to be worth God's attention, and let Jude carry it.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, apostle chosen by Christ, pray for us.

Day 3 of 9

The Name Mistaken for a Traitor's

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

For centuries, the faithful hesitated to call on Jude by name — it sounded too much like Judas, the betrayer, and no one wanted to invoke the wrong one. So devotion to him lagged far behind devotion to the other apostles; he was, in a real sense, the forgotten one. Tradition holds that this very neglect is why he became the saint of last resort, the one people turn to only when every other door has closed. He understands, better than most, what it is to be misjudged, overlooked, or written off before anyone gave the case a fair hearing. Bring him whatever in your life currently feels forgotten or beyond help.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, patron of the forgotten, pray for us.

Day 4 of 9

A Letter on Holding Fast

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

The short New Testament letter traditionally ascribed to Jude was written to a community under strain, being pulled toward error and division. His counsel is not clever argument but steadiness: build yourselves up in faith, pray, keep yourselves in the love of God, and wait — with mercy for those who doubt, but without abandoning what you know to be true. It is a letter for people whose footing feels unsteady. If your situation has you doubting whether to keep holding on, ask Jude today simply for the grace of steadiness — not certainty about the outcome, just the strength to keep standing.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, steadfast in the faith, pray for us.

Day 5 of 9

Carrying Hope to Distant Places

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Ancient accounts, none of them fully certain but all consistent in spirit, send Jude far from home — preaching in Mesopotamia, Syria, Persia, Libya, Armenia: places with little or no prior contact with the Gospel, far from any support he might have relied on. He went anyway, carrying hope into territory where none yet existed. Whatever feels most distant in your own life right now — a resolution that seems impossibly far off, a relationship or circumstance beyond reach — ask him to carry your petition there, the way he once carried the Gospel into unfamiliar and difficult country.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, bearer of hope to distant lands, pray for us.

Day 6 of 9

Bringing Christ's Healing Presence

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

An old tradition, recorded among others by the historian Eusebius, tells of an apostle bringing word — and, in later retellings, an image of Christ — to King Abgar of Edessa, who lay sick with no hope of cure, and who was healed. The details have blurred over centuries of retelling, but the memory fixed itself firmly on Jude: the apostle who carries Christ's healing nearness into the sickroom, into the situation medicine alone cannot fix. This is much of why hospitals claim him as patron. Today, name plainly any illness — your own or someone else's, of body or of spirit — that feels beyond what human help can reach.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, bringer of Christ's healing presence, pray for us.

Day 7 of 9

Faithful to the End

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Tradition holds that Jude, like nearly all the apostles, sealed his witness with martyrdom, by most accounts in Persia, often remembered alongside St. Simon the Zealot — their shared feast falls on October 28. In his final hour he had no way of knowing how the Church would one day remember him, no assurance that his death would mean anything beyond itself. He simply held on to what he had been given. When a cause feels like it is asking everything of you with no guarantee of return, ask Jude for the grace he had: to remain faithful without needing to see how the story ends.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, faithful martyr, pray for us.

Day 8 of 9

Patron of the Desperate

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

Across generations, almost by default, Jude became the saint people reach for only in extremity — the diagnosis with no remaining options, the family torn beyond apparent repair, the job, home, or relationship that looks entirely lost. Not because he intercedes differently than any other saint, but because so many people, at the very end of their own resources, turned specifically to him and found he did not turn away. There is no need to soften your petition today. Name it exactly as it is, however impossible it sounds out loud, and hand it to him without editing it down.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, patron of desperate causes, pray for us.

Day 9 of 9

Thanksgiving and a Promise Kept

Opening prayer — Pray this at the start of each day.

The traditional prayer to St. Jude ends with a promise: to make his name known, so his help never passes unnoticed once it comes. On this final day, give thanks in advance — not because the outcome is yet visible, but because trust offered honestly is already its own grace, whatever happens next. Close this novena by placing your whole petition, and its outcome, into God's hands through Jude's intercession, and resolve, as the old prayer asks, to let others know of the help you received.

Closing prayer

Glory be to the Father, and to the Son, and to the Holy Ghost, as it was in the beginning, is now, and ever shall be, world without end. Amen. St. Jude, pray for us and for all who honor thee and invoke thy aid.

About St. Jude Thaddeus

Jude Thaddeus was one of the Twelve Apostles, called 'Jude of James' in Luke's Gospel and traditionally identified with Thaddeus in Matthew and Mark. Early Christian tradition holds he was a relative of Jesus, son of Cleophas and Mary, and brother of James the Less. He is traditionally credited with missionary journeys through Mesopotamia, Syria, Persia, Libya, and Armenia, and with the New Testament Epistle of Jude, a short letter urging believers to hold fast to the faith. Ancient accounts, recorded by writers such as Eusebius, connect him to the healing of King Abgar of Edessa. He was martyred, by most traditions in Persia, and is venerated on October 28 together with St. Simon. Because his name so closely resembles that of Judas Iscariot, devotion to him lagged for centuries; that very neglect is the traditional root of his patronage of desperate and seemingly hopeless causes — he became the saint people turned to only when everything else had failed, and the devotion that grew from that has become one of the most widespread in the Church, especially since the Claretian Fathers began promoting it at Chicago's National Shrine of St. Jude in 1929.

Biographical details drawn from the Catholic Encyclopedia (1913, public domain), ancient sources including Eusebius's Ecclesiastical History and the Golden Legend, the New Testament (Luke 6:16, Epistle of Jude), and the documented history of the Claretian Fathers' National Shrine of St. Jude, founded in Chicago in 1929. Where ancient tradition is uncertain (e.g. exact missionary itinerary, place and manner of martyrdom), this is noted as tradition rather than settled fact.
